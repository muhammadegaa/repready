# ICP desk research (2026-10-02)

Desk research only. Nothing here comes from a conversation with a club. Where a number is a vendor's own claim or from a global sample, it says so. "Not found" means I searched and did not find a source.

## What the evidence says

**Staffing and pain (52 professional soccer S&C coaches, 18 countries, 38% UK; PMC8329977)**
- Biggest problems: time/scheduling/fixtures 29%, training load and periodisation 27%, buy-in from head coach and colleagues 23%, lack of facilities/staff 15%.
- Quoted problems: "Player:Coach ratio is often a challenge with relatively large playing squads (>20) and low numbers of staff (1–2)". "Managing load in congested fixture schedules between key and bench players". "Differentiation between game-players and reserves".
- How they individualise: by position demands 44% (often via GPS), by screening/wellbeing/testing 33%.
- Technology in use: GPS 94%, heart rate monitors 71%, mobile phone applications 40%, wearable technology 15%.
- With unlimited resources: technology/testing 27% (one example: sleep monitoring), facilities/staffing 27%, individualisation 15%.

**Market size**
- 20 Premier League and 72 English Football League clubs are full-time professional (Wikipedia, English football league system). That is 92 men's first teams.
- Pay in the Premier League is £6.3k, £19k and £21.8k above the Championship, League One and League Two (Sportsmith, 138 practitioners). Lower leagues have leaner staff and budgets. Staff counts per club by league: not found.
- Women's football: Sportsmith surveyed 52 full-time practitioners; 75% work 40 to 60 hours a week. Number of full-time staff per women's club: not found.
- Academies: Kitman Labs and the Premier League announced a centralised platform for all Premier League and EFL academies. Academy monitoring is likely already covered. What the platform includes for wellness and S&C: not confirmed.
- Non-league, college and private S&C coaches: not sized.

**Price anchors (22-player squad; the source is a vendor blog, so treat as indicative)**
- Catapult One Team: $180 per player per year, 2-year commitment, 10-player minimum.
- Kitman Labs: quote only; one aggregator reports a $100,000 base annual subscription.
- Software-only tools: Lumin Sports $600 to $3,350 a year; TeamBuildr Silver plus AMS add-on $1,680 a year; Fractall €200 to €240 a year flat per team.
- Willingness to pay for "wellness check-in plus dashboard" is anchored at a few hundred to a few thousand pounds a year per club.

**Compliance with wellness check-ins (Saw et al., PMC4306765)**
- Team-sport athletes supported by their coach or programme: mean compliance 84 ± 21%.
- Self-directed individual athletes: 28 ± 40%. Self-directed team-sport athletes: 8 ± 18%.
- Other studies cite lack of staff and coach buy-in as the two largest barriers to monitoring, and very long questionnaires as a compliance problem.

**Trust in AI recommendations (literature review level)**
- Coaches struggle to trust recommendations they cannot explain. Transparency and a stated rationale are the repeated requirement. Named rules with edit and approve fit this.

**Women's football**
- WSL clubs use menstrual cycle tracking apps. The FA and Well HQ run an initiative for WSL and Women's Championship clubs. One study found WSL players six times more likely to have a muscle injury in the days before a period. This is real demand, and it is more sensitive data.

**Data protection**
- Health data is special category. A DPIA is usually needed. The club is the controller. Analytics and wearable vendors can become controllers if they use data for their own purposes (benchmarking across clients, product training). RepReady should stay a processor only.

## Candidate ICPs

| ICP | Fit | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| League One and League Two first-team performance staff (1–2 people, 20+ players) | Best first target | The quoted pain is ratio, congestion and game-player versus reserve differentiation. Budget is small, so price must stay low and the tool must save time. |
| Championship first team | Good, harder sale | Same pain, more staff and more existing tools. |
| Women's Championship and WSL | Second wedge | Lean staff, cycle tracking demand. More sensitive data and clinical claims. Budget unknown. |
| Non-league and semi-pro | Low price, fast, unsized | Not researched. |
| Premier League first team | Avoid for now | In-house systems and enterprise procurement. |
| Academies | Avoid for now | Likely covered by the Kitman platform. Under-18 consent and safeguarding. |

## What this research cannot tell us

- Whether any club will pay for an agent that proposes session edits.
- How many lower-league clubs use spreadsheets, a cheap tool or an enterprise system.
- Whether staff would approve the agent's proposals often enough to save time.
