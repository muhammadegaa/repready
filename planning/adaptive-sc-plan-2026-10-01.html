3. Gates to lock

Each gate has a default. Reply "lock all" to accept every default, or name the gate you want to change.

G1 Active bet

Default
Adaptive S&C agent becomes the active bet. Codehere outreach stays parked.
Alternative
Keep codehere active and run this as a side project at a lower hours share.
Why
Two bets at 10–15 hours a week means neither reaches a paying user. Antler explicitly looks for commitment with no side projects.

G2 First buyer

Default
UK school, college and lower-league club S&C coaches, adult athletes only at first.
Alternative
UK personal trainers (about 25,000 businesses, larger but crowded at $9–£52 a month).
Why
The team-coach slot is the open one. The cofounder's credibility sits with S&C coaches. hypothesis

G3 Product shape

Default
Agent proposes, coach approves, athlete sees the adjusted session. Nothing applies without approval.
Alternative
Athlete-facing autonomous adjustment, like WHOOP Coach or JuggernautAI.
Why
The coach is accountable for the athlete, so approval is the trust mechanism. It also avoids competing with consumer apps on their ground.

G4 Wearables in the first build

Default
No. Inputs are RPE, sleep, soreness, stress and a note. The readiness_signal table exists so wearables plug in later.
Alternative
Include one aggregator from week 1 (Terra at $399 a month, or Junction at about $0.50 per user per month with a $300 minimum).
Why
That fixed cost starts before any revenue, and the evidence for wearable readiness in strength work is thin (section 5). Add it once a paying pilot names the device its athletes use.

G5 First price to test

Default
£79 a month for up to 50 athletes, unlimited coaches.
Alternative
Per-athlete pricing from £1.50 an athlete.
Why
Sits between Volt (cheapest in the S&C guide) and TeamBuildr ($90–280). hypothesis The payment link is the test.

G6 Distribution

Default
The cofounder publishes evidence-based posts on auto-regulation for S&C coaches. Each links to the landing page.
Alternative
Cold outreach to coaches. Excluded by your "no asking people" rule.
Why
Public posts from a PhD are also the credibility signal investors ask about. At 3 hours a week the cofounder cannot write posts, so I draft and they review and publish, about 30 minutes a week.

G7 Pass bar for the agent

Default
At least 80% agreement with the cofounder on 30 scenarios, and 100% on the 8 "do nothing" cases.
Alternative
A different threshold, or agreement measured against a second sports scientist.
Why
It turns "works" into a check that can fail. The 80% figure is our choice, not a published standard.

5. Evidence, and its limits

Problem

  • TeamBuildr coach side described as "cumbersome and counter-intuitive", with editing, copying and pasting movements "far too difficult". sourced Excerpt only, the review sites returned HTTP 403.
  • TeamBuildr iOS app: 3.2 out of 5 from 260 ratings, and release notes show repeated crash, sign-out, offline and messaging fixes. sourced
  • TrainHeroic: crashes, slow loads, entries that sometimes do not save. sourced
  • Not evidenced: that coaches want automatic adjustment, or will pay for it. This is the central hypothesis.

Science

  • HRV-guided training: small positive effect in endurance athletes, VO2max effect size about 0.40, varying by athlete level and sex. sourced
  • No strength-specific equivalent found. open The cofounder should supply it.
  • Claims allowed: time saved, earlier visibility of overload. Claims not allowed yet: better performance, injury prevention, anything medical.

6. Build plan

Builder about 12 hours a week, cofounder 3 hours a week. Each week ends at a gate. The cofounder reviews and labels drafts instead of writing from scratch: I draft the candidate rules and scenarios from the cited literature, and the cofounder's edits and decisions are the ground truth. Risk: reviewing a draft can anchor the expert toward it. Mitigation: the cofounder writes each scenario's decision before reading the drafted one.

flowchart LR
  A[Coach imports program] --> B[Athlete daily check-in]
  B --> C[Agent drafts edits + reasons]
  C --> D{Coach decision}
  D -->|approve or edit| E[Athlete sees adjusted session]
  D -->|reject| F[Planned session unchanged]
  E --> G[Audit log]
  F --> G

Stack: Next.js, TypeScript, Tailwind, Supabase, Anthropic SDK, Vercel, Stripe. Hard limits are enforced in code, not in the prompt: no load above plan, volume change capped at ±25%, no edits on flagged injuries without coach clearance, no medical language.

Hours check. At 3 hours a week the cofounder's work is about 9 hours over three weeks. My estimate is 10 to 15 minutes per scenario to label, plus rule editing. That makes this a five-week build to a live payment link, not three. The first-money test moves from week 3 to week 5. If the cofounder can give 6 hours a week, weeks 1 to 3 compress to 2.

7. Kill criteria

  • The cofounder cannot write at least 10 distinct, evidence-backed adjustment rules for strength sessions in one sitting.
  • The agent agrees with the cofounder on less than 80% of the 30 scenarios after two prompt iterations.
  • The landing page, with live content distribution, gets no paid deposit or pre-order within 30 days.
